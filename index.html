Potato

No matter what I do I cannot escape the Potato. Everything I learn about them makes me more and more fascinated with how this one vegetable can provide so much nutrition with so little downsides. I have yet to find a perfect food and I don’t think there is such thing as a perfect naturally occurring food but the Potato is very high up on the list.

First of all, the nutritional content of a good potato is very impressive. They are an almost complete food nutritionally, containing all essential amino acids, excellent vitamin C and B6, and potassium as well as other minerals. While potatoes don't contain every vitamin (they're missing B12, A, D, and E), they come closer to nutritional completeness than nearly any other single food.

Source: USDA FoodData Central (FDC ID 170026). Values for 500g potato, raw with skin (approximately 385 calories).

Nutritional Completeness Comparison — 2500 Calories

Each square represents one essential nutrient. Darker = more complete. See how potato compares to rice as a single-food staple.

Pure Potato Diet (2500 cal)
Pure Rice Diet (2500 cal)

Each cell represents one of 40+ essential nutrients. Brightness shows % of RDA met (0-25% = lightest, 75%+ = darkest). Notice: rice has MANY light/empty squares (missing Vitamin C, A, D, E, K, B12 and deficient in most minerals). Potato has only 3 missing nutrients (B12, A, D) but provides substantial amounts of nearly everything else—demonstrating true nutritional completeness from a single food.

Realistic Minimal Diets — 2500 Calories

Adding just 1.2L low-fat milk + sunlight makes both more realistic. See how salted potato becomes nearly complete while rice still has major gaps.

Salted Potato + 1.2L Milk + Sun
Rice + 1.2L Milk + Sun

Both diets: 2000 cal base food + 500 cal low-fat milk (1.2L) + 30 min daily sun exposure. Salted potato + milk + sun = only 1 minor deficiency (Vitamin A at ~67% RDA). Rice + milk + sun = still missing Vitamin C, E, K entirely and deficient in potassium, magnesium, and other minerals. To make potato diet 100% complete: Add ~50g cheese (fills Vitamin A, boosts K) + small amount of greens broth boiled long to remove antinutrients (fills Vitamin K). This creates a nutritionally complete diet from just 4-5 whole foods.
